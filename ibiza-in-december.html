Ibiza in December

Lights on in the villages

NovemberDecemberJanuaryFebruaryMarchApril

December is when the island stops pretending it's a resort. The Christmas lights go on in four towns, the markets move from the hippy fields to the boulevards, the choirs tour the parish churches, and on Christmas morning people you know go for a swim before lunch. It is short days, long lunches and the kind of quiet you can't buy in August.

This guide is written by people who live here year-round. Everything below links to the live agenda, the Open in Winter list and the markets page, which are updated every week, so the dates on this page are this year's, not last year's. Town halls publish their Christmas programmes through November; we add them the day they appear.

Weather in Ibiza in December

The first proper winter month, by Ibiza standards: bright, breezy and 16 degrees in the afternoon, with cold clear nights after a front passes. Days are short — the sun is down by half past five — so plan the walk for the middle of the day and the lunch for after it. The sea is colder than November but still swimmable for the brave.

Two things to know: the tramuntana blows hardest this month, which decides which side of the island you walk or swim on (the beaches guide sorts the coves by wind), and a sunny December day in a sheltered cove is genuinely T-shirt weather at lunchtime. Evenings are for a coat and a fire.

What makes December special

The lights go on

Ibiza Town switches on first, at the end of November, with the Christmas market on Vara de Rey opening the same week. Santa Eulària, Sant Antoni and Sant Josep follow in the first days of December. The villages do their own version: a tree on the church square, a string of bulbs along the bar.

Four Christmas markets

Vara de Rey in Ibiza Town runs daily until Three Kings; Santa Eulària's takes over the Rambla; Sant Antoni's fills the fountain square in the evenings and all weekend; and Las Dalias turns its hippy market into a Christmas one on December weekends, with mulled wine and the same forty-odd stalls. Dates and hours for all four are on the markets page. The Saturday farmers' circuit — Forada, Las Dalias, Juntos Farm — keeps going right through the month.

The Puente (6–8 December)

Constitution Day on the 6th and the Immaculate Conception on the 8th make a long weekend that half of Spain spends travelling. On Ibiza it's the busiest December weekend: restaurants full, the markets at their liveliest, the first Nadal concerts. Book the Sunday lunch.

Carols, choirs and the Caramelles

Through December the island's choirs sing nadales in the parish churches and the Dalt Vila convent, free and candlelit. On Christmas Eve, the oldest tradition of all: the Caramelles de Nadal, medieval verses sung at midnight mass in villages like Sant Miquel, Sant Josep and Santa Gertrudis, with flute, drum and castanets. It is the most Ibicencan thing you will ever hear, and visitors are welcome.

Christmas Day and Sant Esteve

The 25th is a family day; the island is closed and quiet, and the beaches are where everyone ends up mid-morning. The 26th, Sant Esteve, is a holiday too, and the day the restaurants reopen for the second big lunch of the week.

Cap d'Any and the Sant Silvestre

New Year's Eve starts in trainers: the Sant Silvestre fun run through Ibiza Town in the late afternoon, in fancy dress. Then the twelve grapes at midnight on Vara de Rey or the village square, cava, and the first swim of the year on the 1st if you're that kind of person. Clubs do open for the night; the Agenda will say which.

The Kings come by boat (5 January)

The month ends with the island's best spectacle: the Three Kings arriving by boat in Ibiza harbour, Santa Eulària and Sant Antoni on the evening of 5 January, then a cavalcade through town throwing sweets. If you're on the island over New Year, stay for it.

Olives, oranges and the first sobrassada

In the countryside December is the olive harvest; the few working tafones press the island's oil now, and the oranges and mandarins come in. The matances continue, and the first fresh sobrassada of the season appears at the markets. The Winter Table has where to taste all of it.

Ten things to do in Ibiza in December

1

Do all four Christmas markets in one weekend

Vara de Rey on Friday night, Las Dalias on Saturday morning, Santa Eulària's Rambla on Saturday afternoon, Sant Antoni's fountain square on Sunday evening. Mulled wine at each one.

2

Hear the choirs in a village church

Free nadales concerts in the parish churches through the month; the Dalt Vila convent is the atmospheric one. Dates on the agenda as the councils publish them.

3

Swim on Christmas morning

Seventeen degrees, a sheltered cove, ten minutes in and a flask of coffee after. Half the island does it; the other half watches from the bar.

4

Walk in the short, golden light

December light is the year's best for the cliff paths: Ses Portes del Cel above Santa Agnès, or the Sa Talaia summit from Sant Josep. Start at eleven, finish by four.

5

Book a long lunch by a fire

The villages' restaurants with a log fire are December's real destination: Santa Gertrudis, Sant Mateu, Sant Miquel. See the Winter Table, or ask us for a table.

6

Go to midnight mass for the Caramelles

24 December, Sant Miquel or Sant Josep. Arrive early, sit at the back, and listen to verses that have been sung here for five hundred years.

7

Find the live music

Blues at Can Jordi, Thursday nights at Cas Costas, jazz and vinyl at Las Dalias Café. December weekends are the busiest of the winter for the small stages.

8

Run the Sant Silvestre

31 December, late afternoon, Ibiza Town, costumes encouraged. Five kilometres, then the grapes.

9

Spa day on a grey day

Can Curreu and Can Lluc keep their spas open all winter; a hammam afternoon is the island's answer to a wet Tuesday.

10

Wait for the Kings

5 January, the harbour at dusk: the boats come in, the Kings climb onto the quay, and the cavalcade starts. The best night of a child's year on Ibiza.

Forada market before the bread runs out

Retreats in Ibiza in December

The quiet month for retreats: organisers take Christmas off and the fincas close for family. A few day workshops and sound journeys run in the first half of the month, then the calendar picks up again in January and gets busy from February, when the almond blossom retreats start. The retreats page has every one we've confirmed, from licensed venues only.

Ibiza in December with kids

  • The Christmas markets all have a children's corner — the carousel and the pista de hielo (a small ice rink) on Vara de Rey are the December fixtures.
  • Betlems (nativity scenes): every town hall and most churches build one; Santa Eulària's and Sant Antoni's are the elaborate ones, and there is usually a living nativity in one of the villages in the week before Christmas.
  • Father Christmas and the Kings' postman visit the markets on set afternoons to collect letters — dates on the agenda.
  • Sant Silvestre has a children's distance and a prize for the best costume.
  • 5 January: the Kings arrive by boat and throw sweets from the floats. Be on the harbour front by 17:30.
  • Rainy day: the Madina Yabisa museum in Dalt Vila, the aquarium at Cap Blanc, or a children's film at Teatro España.

What's open in Ibiza in December

More than November, oddly: the Christmas markets and the Puente bring the island's own people out, and restaurants that shut for a November holiday reopen for the season. Ibiza Town, Santa Eulària, Sant Josep and Santa Gertrudis are fully open; the villages keep a bar and a restaurant or two each. On the beaches, La Escollera at Es Cavallet, Yemanjá at Cala Jondal and Sa Caleta do Christmas lunches by the fire, and Hostal La Torre does the sunset. The rural hotels with spas — Can Curreu, Can Lluc — stay open; most of the beach-resort strip does not.

Closed days to plan around: 25 December almost everything, 1 January until the afternoon, 6 January (Three Kings) a holiday with shops shut. The full list, village by village with winter days and Christmas hours, is Open in Winter.

Ibiza in December — quick answers

Is Ibiza worth visiting in December?

Yes, for a quiet, sunny, walkable island with Christmas markets, village traditions and restaurants without a wait. Not for clubbing or beach-bar days; the superclubs are shut until spring and most beach bars with them.

What's the weather like in Ibiza at Christmas?

Usually bright: 15 to 17°C in the afternoon, 8 or 9°C at night, a windy day or two a week. Christmas Day itself is a lottery between a lunch outside in the sun and a fire indoors; pack for both.

Is Ibiza open at Christmas?

The towns are. Ibiza Town and Santa Eulària trade normally except on 25 December and 1 January; markets run daily. Our Open in Winter list has the places with their Christmas hours.

Can you swim in Ibiza in December?

The sea is about 17°C — cold but doable, and a Christmas-morning dip is a local tradition. Pick a cove sheltered from the day's wind and go in at midday.

What happens in Ibiza on New Year's Eve?

The Sant Silvestre fun run in the afternoon, the twelve grapes at midnight on Vara de Rey, and a handful of club and hotel parties — fewer than you'd think, and the good ones sell out early. Then the Kings on 5 January.
